# Learning Environment and Technology Requirements

## 13.1 Purpose

Successful implementation of the Artificial Intelligence Curriculum
Framework depends upon providing learners with a safe, inclusive, and
well-supported learning environment that encourages exploration,
creativity, collaboration, and responsible use of emerging technologies.

The learning environment should enable learners to investigate AI
concepts through authentic experiences while ensuring equitable access
to appropriate digital tools and resources.

Although the curriculum references a range of AI platforms and
technologies, the emphasis remains on developing transferable knowledge,
skills, and understanding rather than proficiency with any single
application.

Schools may adapt the recommended technologies to suit local
infrastructure, safeguarding policies, and available resources.

## 13.2 Learning Environment

The learning environment should promote:

- curiosity and inquiry;

- creativity and innovation;

- collaboration and discussion;

- experimentation and iterative improvement;

- critical evaluation of AI outputs;

- responsible and ethical technology use.

Learners should be encouraged to investigate problems, test ideas,
analyse evidence, and reflect upon their learning in an environment
where questioning and constructive risk-taking are valued.

## 13.3 Minimum Technology Requirements

The curriculum is designed to be accessible using widely available
technologies.

The recommended minimum requirements include:

| **Component**   | **Recommendation**                                              |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Student Device  | One internet-connected computer or laptop per learner           |
| Internet Access | Reliable broadband connection suitable for cloud-based AI tools |
| Web Browser     | Current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari             |
| Display         | Interactive display or projector for whole-class modelling      |
| Audio           | Speakers or headphones where appropriate                        |
| Storage         | Cloud-based storage for learner portfolios and project work     |

## 13.4 Recommended Digital Platforms

The curriculum is platform-flexible; however, the following tools are
recommended because they align well with the learning outcomes and
progression model.

| **Purpose**                    | **Suggested Platforms**                    |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Generative AI                  | ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini  |
| Machine Learning               | Google Teachable Machine                   |
| Productivity and Collaboration | Google Workspace or Microsoft 365          |
| Data Analysis (Y7–9)           | Google Sheets, Microsoft Excel             |
| Programming (Y7–9)             | Google Colab                               |
| Presentation                   | Google Slides, Microsoft PowerPoint, Canva |
| Documentation                  | Google Docs or Microsoft Word              |

Schools may substitute equivalent platforms provided that learning
objectives are maintained.

## 13.5 Technology Progression

The complexity of technology use increases throughout the curriculum.

| **Phase** | **Technology Focus**                                                                            |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Years 3–4 | Guided interaction with browser-based AI tools and simple machine learning activities           |
| Years 5–6 | Independent use of AI tools, prompt refinement, and introductory machine learning models        |
| Years 7–8 | Structured AI workflows, data analysis, Google Colab, and introductory Python                   |
| Year 9    | Independent investigation, workflow design, model evaluation, and advanced AI-assisted projects |

This progression reflects increasing learner independence while
maintaining appropriate teacher guidance.

## 13.6 Classroom Organisation

The curriculum is designed to support a range of teaching approaches.

Teachers may use:

- whole-class instruction for introducing new concepts;

- paired activities for prompt engineering and AI investigations;

- collaborative group work for project development;

- independent tasks for research, reflection, and assessment.

Flexible grouping encourages peer learning while allowing learners to
develop independence as they progress through the curriculum.

## 13.7 Digital Safety and Safeguarding

Schools should ensure that the use of AI technologies aligns with local
safeguarding, online safety, and acceptable use policies.

When using AI platforms, learners should be reminded to:

- protect personal information;

- avoid sharing sensitive data;

- use respectful and appropriate language;

- report unexpected or inappropriate outputs to the teacher;

- recognise that AI-generated information should be verified.

Teachers should monitor AI interactions and select age-appropriate
platforms that comply with school safeguarding requirements.

## 13.8 Accessibility and Inclusion

The curriculum is designed to be accessible to learners with diverse
needs and abilities.

Teachers should consider:

- multiple ways for learners to access information;

- opportunities for collaborative learning;

- flexible methods for demonstrating understanding;

- scaffolded support where appropriate;

- extension opportunities for learners requiring additional challenge.

The use of AI should support inclusion by providing alternative methods
for communication, organisation, and creativity where appropriate.

## 13.9 Digital Portfolio

Learners are encouraged to maintain a digital portfolio throughout the
programme.

The portfolio may include:

- screenshots of AI interactions;

- prompt iterations;

- machine learning investigations;

- project documentation;

- reflections;

- presentations;

- assessment evidence.

Digital portfolios provide a valuable record of learner progression and
support both formative and summative assessment.

## 13.10 Resource Planning by Phase

| **Phase** | **Core Resources**                                                                       |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Years 3–4 | Browser-based AI tools, images, videos, simple datasets, collaborative activities        |
| Years 5–6 | Teachable Machine, generative AI tools, structured datasets, presentation software       |
| Years 7–8 | Google Colab, spreadsheets, AI tools, curated datasets, workflow templates               |
| Year 9    | Google Colab, Python notebooks, AI platforms, authentic datasets, project planning tools |

## 13.11 Sustainability and Future-Proofing

Artificial intelligence technologies continue to evolve rapidly.

For this reason, the curriculum focuses on enduring concepts,
transferable skills, and responsible practices rather than dependence on
specific software platforms.

Schools are encouraged to review recommended technologies periodically
and adopt equivalent tools where appropriate, ensuring that the
curriculum remains current while preserving its educational intent.

## 13.12 Summary

A well-designed learning environment enables learners to explore AI
confidently, safely, and responsibly. By combining appropriate
technology, effective classroom organisation, and inclusive teaching
practices, schools can create rich learning experiences that support
both technical development and ethical understanding.
