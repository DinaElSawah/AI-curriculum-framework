# 1. Introduction


Artificial Intelligence (AI) is rapidly transforming the way individuals
learn, work, communicate, and solve problems. As AI technologies become
increasingly embedded within everyday life, learners require more than a
basic awareness of digital tools; they need the knowledge, skills, and
understanding to interact with intelligent systems confidently,
critically, and responsibly.

The Artificial Intelligence Curriculum Framework (Years 3–9) provides a
structured and progressive learning pathway that enables learners to
develop foundational AI literacy while building the competencies
required to participate effectively in an increasingly AI-enabled world.

The curriculum is designed as an AI strand within the wider STEAM
programme, integrating concepts from science, technology, engineering,
mathematics, and creative problem-solving. Through practical
exploration, critical inquiry, and authentic interaction with AI
systems, learners gradually develop an understanding of how intelligent
technologies function, how they are applied in real-world contexts, and
how they influence individuals, communities, and society.

Rather than focusing solely on the use of AI tools, the curriculum
encourages learners to investigate how AI systems are developed,
trained, evaluated, and improved. Learners explore the opportunities and
challenges associated with AI while developing the technical
understanding, ethical awareness, and problem-solving capabilities
needed to navigate future technological developments.

The framework adopts a spiral progression model in which key concepts
are revisited throughout Years 3–9 with increasing complexity and
independence. Learning is organised across four interconnected domains:

- **AI Recognition & Understanding (RU)**

- **Machine Learning & Data (ML)**

- **Prompt Engineering & AI Interaction (PE)**

- **Ethics & Societal Impact (ES)**

Together, these domains support the development of learners who can
understand AI systems, interact with them effectively, evaluate their
outputs critically, and apply AI technologies responsibly and
creatively.

## 1.2 Vision Statement


The Artificial Intelligence Curriculum Framework aims to develop
informed, responsible, and future-ready learners who can understand,
evaluate, and apply artificial intelligence technologies with
confidence, creativity, and ethical awareness.

The curriculum seeks to empower learners not only as users of AI
technologies but also as critical thinkers, problem-solvers, and
innovators capable of making informed decisions about the role of AI in
society.

Through progressively challenging learning experiences, learners develop
the knowledge, skills, and dispositions required to engage with
intelligent systems, evaluate their impact, and contribute positively to
a future in which AI will play an increasingly significant role in
education, employment, and everyday life.

The curriculum promotes a balanced understanding of AI by recognising
both its potential benefits and its limitations. Learners are encouraged
to explore AI as a powerful tool for learning, creativity,
communication, and problem-solving while maintaining an awareness of
issues such as bias, fairness, privacy, accountability, and responsible
use.

Ultimately, the framework aims to cultivate learners who are adaptable,
reflective, and prepared to participate successfully in an AI-driven
future.

## 1.3 Curriculum Aims


The curriculum aims to enable learners to:

### Understanding and Knowledge


- develop an understanding of how AI systems function and learn from
  data;

- recognise the role of AI technologies in everyday life and across
  different sectors;

- understand the processes involved in training, testing, and evaluating
  AI systems;

- develop knowledge of key AI concepts, terminology, and applications.

### Skills and Application


- interact effectively with AI systems using clear and purposeful
  inputs;

- apply computational thinking strategies to investigate and solve
  problems;

- use AI tools to support creativity, research, communication, and
  innovation;

- evaluate AI-generated outputs and make informed judgements about their
  reliability and usefulness;

- design increasingly sophisticated AI-supported solutions to real-world
  challenges.

### Ethics and Responsibility


- recognise ethical issues associated with the development and use of AI
  technologies;

- evaluate the impact of AI on individuals, communities, and society;

- understand the importance of fairness, privacy, transparency, and
  accountability;

- use AI technologies responsibly, safely, and ethically.

### Future Readiness


- develop adaptability and confidence when working with emerging
  technologies;

- build skills that support future learning and career pathways;

- understand how AI is transforming industries and workplaces;

- prepare for further study in Computing, Computer Science, Data
  Science, Engineering, and related disciplines.

## 1.4 Curriculum Rationale


Artificial intelligence is increasingly recognised as one of the
defining technologies of the twenty-first century. It is transforming
industries, influencing decision-making processes, shaping digital
experiences, and creating new opportunities across almost every sector
of society.

As AI technologies continue to evolve, AI literacy is becoming an
essential component of modern education. Learners require opportunities
to understand how intelligent systems operate, how they influence the
information they encounter, and how they can be used responsibly to
solve problems and create value.

The rationale for this curriculum is founded upon three key principles:

### Future Workforce Readiness


The future workforce will increasingly require individuals who can work
alongside intelligent systems, interpret data, evaluate automated
outputs, and adapt to rapidly changing technological environments.

While not all learners will pursue careers directly related to
artificial intelligence, many will encounter AI technologies throughout
their future education, employment, and daily lives. Developing AI
literacy therefore supports broader employability skills including
critical thinking, communication, creativity, collaboration,
adaptability, and problem-solving.

The curriculum provides learners with a foundation that supports future
pathways in Computing, Engineering, Data Science, Digital Technologies,
and other emerging disciplines while also developing transferable skills
applicable across a wide range of professions.

### Strong Ethical Integration


The increasing influence of AI technologies requires learners to
understand not only how systems function but also how they affect
individuals and society.

Ethical considerations are therefore embedded throughout the curriculum
rather than being taught as isolated topics.

Learners explore issues including:

- fairness and bias,

- privacy and data protection,

- misinformation,

- intellectual property,

- accountability,

- responsible innovation,

- societal impact.

This approach supports the development of informed digital citizens who
can engage thoughtfully and responsibly with emerging technologies.

### Practical AI Tool Usage


AI is best understood through meaningful interaction and application.

The curriculum therefore emphasises authentic engagement with
age-appropriate AI technologies, enabling learners to explore concepts
through practical experience rather than theoretical study alone.

Learners interact with AI systems, test machine learning models, refine
prompts, evaluate outputs, and apply AI tools to real-world challenges.

Through this hands-on approach, learners develop both conceptual
understanding and practical competence while building confidence in the
responsible use of AI technologies.

### The Role of AI Within STEAM Education


As an AI strand within the STEAM programme, the curriculum promotes
interdisciplinary learning and encourages learners to draw connections
between science, technology, engineering, mathematics, creativity, and
innovation.

AI provides a powerful context through which learners can apply
knowledge, investigate problems, analyse data, design solutions, and
evaluate outcomes.

This integrated approach supports deeper learning and reflects the
increasingly interconnected nature of modern technological challenges.

# Curriculum Design Principles


The Artificial Intelligence Curriculum Framework is guided by six core
design principles that inform curriculum planning, teaching, learning,
assessment, and progression.

These principles ensure that learning experiences remain purposeful,
coherent, and aligned with the needs of contemporary learners.

## 3.1 Human-Centred AI


The curriculum recognises that AI systems are created, trained, and
managed by people.

Learners are encouraged to understand AI as a tool designed to support
human goals rather than replace human judgement.

Learning experiences emphasise the relationship between people and
technology, helping learners recognise that responsible decision-making
remains a fundamentally human responsibility.

## 3.2 Responsible and Ethical Use


Ethical understanding is embedded throughout the curriculum.

Learners are encouraged to evaluate the consequences of AI systems and
consider how technologies can be developed and used responsibly.

Responsible use includes consideration of:

- fairness;

- privacy;

- transparency;

- accountability;

- safety;

- societal impact.

## 3.3 Learning Through Application


Learners develop understanding most effectively when concepts are
explored through meaningful application.

The curriculum therefore prioritises:

- practical investigation;

- experimentation;

- AI tool interaction;

- collaborative discussion;

- project-based learning;

- reflection.

Learning experiences move beyond passive knowledge acquisition and
encourage active engagement with AI systems.

## 3.4 Progressive Technical Development


Technical complexity increases gradually throughout the curriculum.

Learners first develop conceptual understanding before engaging with
increasingly sophisticated ideas such as:

- classification;

- training and testing;

- model evaluation;

- structured prompting;

- data analysis;

- introductory Python;

- workflow design.

This progression ensures accessibility while maintaining appropriate
challenge.

## 3.5 Critical Evaluation


AI systems are not infallible.

Learners are encouraged to question outputs, identify limitations,
evaluate reliability, and consider alternative perspectives.

This principle supports the development of critical thinking and helps
learners become informed and responsible users of AI technologies.

## 3.6 Future Readiness


The curriculum has been designed to prepare learners for continued
technological change.

Rather than focusing on specific software tools, the framework
prioritises transferable knowledge, skills, and dispositions that remain
relevant as technologies evolve.

Learners develop confidence, adaptability, curiosity, and resilience
while gaining practical experience with emerging technologies.

These qualities support future learning, future employment, and lifelong
engagement with AI-enabled environments.
