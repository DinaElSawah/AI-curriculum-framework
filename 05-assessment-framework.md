# Assessment Framework

## 9.1 Assessment Philosophy

Assessment within the Artificial Intelligence Curriculum Framework is
designed to support learning, inform teaching, and provide meaningful
evidence of learner progress.

The curriculum recognises that effective AI education extends beyond
factual recall and requires learners to demonstrate understanding,
application, evaluation, ethical awareness, and reflection.

Assessment therefore focuses on what learners can understand, do,
create, and explain when engaging with AI systems and concepts.

Assessment activities are designed to:

- monitor progress over time;

- provide opportunities for feedback and improvement;

- encourage critical thinking and reflection;

- support creativity and innovation;

- evaluate both technical and ethical understanding;

- celebrate learner growth and achievement.

Assessment is viewed as an ongoing process rather than a single event
and is integrated throughout learning experiences.

## 9.2 Principles of Assessment

Assessment within the curriculum is guided by the following principles.

### Authentic

Assessment tasks should reflect realistic uses of AI technologies and
encourage meaningful application of knowledge and skills.

### Developmental

Assessment should support progression and recognise improvement over
time.

### Balanced

Assessment should evaluate both technical understanding and responsible
use of AI technologies.

### Reflective

Learners should be encouraged to explain decisions, evaluate outcomes,
and identify areas for improvement.

### Inclusive

Assessment opportunities should allow all learners to demonstrate
understanding through a variety of approaches and formats.

## 9.3 Assessment Structure

Assessment is divided into two components.

| **Component**      | **Weighting** |
|--------------------|---------------|
| Ongoing Assessment | 70%           |
| Final Project      | 30%           |

This balance reflects the curriculum’s emphasis on continuous learning,
practical engagement, and iterative improvement.

## 9.4 Ongoing Assessment (70%)

Ongoing assessment takes place throughout the learning process and
provides evidence of developing understanding and skills.

Teachers may gather evidence through:

- practical investigations;

- AI tool interaction tasks;

- prompt design activities;

- machine learning experiments;

- discussions and questioning;

- written reflections;

- collaborative activities;

- presentations;

- digital portfolios;

- quizzes and knowledge checks.

The emphasis should remain on evidence of learning rather than task
completion alone.

## 9.5 Final Project Assessment (30%)

Each year group completes a prescribed final project aligned with the
curriculum progression pathway.

Projects provide opportunities for learners to integrate knowledge and
skills from all four curriculum domains.

Projects should require learners to:

- investigate a problem or challenge;

- apply AI concepts and tools;

- evaluate outcomes;

- consider ethical implications;

- communicate findings effectively.

## 9.6 Assessment Strands

All assessment tasks should align with one or more of the following
strands.

### Technical Competency

Evaluates the learner’s understanding and application of AI concepts,
systems, tools, and processes.

Examples include:

- identifying AI systems;

- training models;

- evaluating outputs;

- using AI tools effectively.

### Communication

Evaluates the learner’s ability to explain ideas, present findings,
justify decisions, and communicate with both humans and AI systems.

Examples include:

- prompt construction;

- presentations;

- written explanations;

- project reports.

### Ethical Reasoning

Evaluates the learner’s understanding of responsible AI use and ability
to consider ethical implications.

Examples include:

- discussions of bias;

- privacy considerations;

- fairness analysis;

- responsible use decisions.

### Reflection

Evaluates the learner’s ability to review outcomes, identify
improvements, and explain learning processes.

Examples include:

- self-assessment;

- project evaluations;

- iterative improvement records;

- learning journals.

## 9.7 Achievement Descriptors

The curriculum uses four achievement levels to describe learner
performance.

These descriptors apply across all year groups while expectations
increase according to developmental stage.

### Emerging

The learner demonstrates limited understanding and requires significant
support to apply concepts and skills.

Typical characteristics:

- recognises some key ideas;

- follows instructions with support;

- demonstrates developing awareness of concepts;

- requires guidance to complete tasks.

### Developing

The learner demonstrates growing understanding and can apply concepts
with occasional support.

Typical characteristics:

- explains key concepts;

- applies skills in familiar situations;

- completes tasks with increasing independence;

- identifies simple strengths and areas for improvement.

### Secure

The learner demonstrates consistent understanding and applies knowledge
independently.

Typical characteristics:

- explains concepts accurately;

- applies skills confidently;

- evaluates outcomes effectively;

- demonstrates responsible decision-making.

### Mastering

The learner demonstrates advanced understanding and applies learning
flexibly in new contexts.

Typical characteristics:

- analyses concepts critically;

- applies learning creatively;

- evaluates systems and solutions in depth;

- demonstrates strong reflection and ethical awareness.

## 9.8 Assessment Progression Across Phases

| **Phase** | **Assessment Focus**                           |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------|
| Years 3–4 | Recognition, explanation, participation        |
| Years 5–6 | Application, testing, improvement              |
| Years 7–8 | Design, analysis, problem-solving              |
| Year 9    | Evaluation, justification, critical reflection |

Assessment expectations increase in complexity and independence
throughout the curriculum.

## 9.9 Project Assessment Framework

The following framework should be used when assessing final projects.

| **Strand**           | **Suggested Weighting** |
|----------------------|-------------------------|
| Technical Competency | 40%                     |
| Communication        | 20%                     |
| Ethical Reasoning    | 20%                     |
| Reflection           | 20%                     |

This model ensures that projects evaluate both technical understanding
and responsible participation.

## 9.11 Assessment and Reporting

Assessment information should be used to:

- provide feedback to learners;

- inform future teaching and learning;

- monitor progress over time;

- support reporting to parents and school leaders;

- identify areas requiring additional support or challenge.

Achievement descriptors should be used consistently across all year
groups to ensure continuity and progression.

## 9.12 Assessment Summary

The assessment framework supports the curriculum’s broader goal of
developing learners who can:

- understand AI systems;

- apply AI tools effectively;

- evaluate outputs critically;

- act responsibly and ethically;

- reflect on learning and improvement.

By balancing technical understanding with ethical awareness and
reflective practice, assessment becomes an integral part of preparing
learners for future participation in an increasingly AI-enabled world.
