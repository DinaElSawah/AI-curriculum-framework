# Responsible Use of Generative AI

## 10.1 Purpose

Generative Artificial Intelligence tools are becoming increasingly
accessible and influential within education, workplaces, and society.

These technologies offer significant opportunities to support learning,
creativity, communication, productivity, and problem-solving. However,
they also present challenges relating to accuracy, bias, privacy,
intellectual property, and responsible use.

The Artificial Intelligence Curriculum Framework promotes the
purposeful, safe, and ethical use of generative AI technologies
throughout all year groups.

Learners are encouraged to view AI systems as tools that can support
human thinking and creativity rather than replace human judgement,
responsibility, or effort.

The purpose of this section is to establish a consistent approach to the
responsible use of generative AI across teaching, learning, and
assessment activities.

## 10.2 Guiding Principles

The use of generative AI within the curriculum is guided by the
following principles.

### Human Judgement Remains Essential

AI-generated outputs should support human thinking rather than replace
it.

Learners should be encouraged to question, evaluate, verify, and improve
AI-generated content.

Final decisions, interpretations, and conclusions remain the
responsibility of the learner.

### Transparency

Learners should understand when AI tools have been used to support
learning, research, creation, or problem-solving activities.

Appropriate acknowledgement of AI-supported work should be encouraged.

### Responsibility

Learners should use AI technologies in ways that are respectful, safe,
ethical, and aligned with school expectations.

AI tools should never be used to generate harmful, discriminatory,
misleading, or inappropriate content.

### Critical Evaluation

AI systems are not always accurate.

Learners should develop habits of verification and evidence-based
evaluation rather than assuming AI-generated outputs are correct.

## 10.3 Understanding AI Limitations

Generative AI systems are powerful but imperfect.

Learners should understand that AI systems:

- may generate inaccurate information;

- may produce outdated information;

- may demonstrate bias;

- may misunderstand prompts;

- may generate incomplete responses;

- may present information confidently even when incorrect.

Developing awareness of these limitations is a key aspect of AI
literacy.

## 10.4 Verification of AI Outputs

Learners should be taught to verify important information before
accepting it as accurate.

Verification strategies may include:

- consulting trusted sources;

- comparing multiple sources of information;

- checking dates and context;

- evaluating evidence;

- discussing findings with teachers and peers.

The curriculum promotes the principle:

“Trust, but verify.”

Learners should understand that responsible use of AI involves checking
information rather than accepting outputs without question.

## 10.5 Bias and Fairness

AI systems learn from data.

As a result, biases present within training data may influence outputs
generated by AI systems.

Throughout the curriculum, learners investigate:

- how bias can occur;

- how representation affects outcomes;

- why fairness is important;

- how biased outputs may affect individuals and communities.

Learners are encouraged to recognise that fairness in AI is an ongoing
challenge that requires human oversight and critical evaluation.

## 10.6 Privacy and Data Protection

Learners should understand the importance of protecting personal
information when using digital technologies.

When interacting with AI systems, learners should avoid sharing:

- personal addresses;

- passwords;

- financial information;

- sensitive personal data;

- private information about themselves or others.

Teachers should reinforce safe digital practices and ensure that
learners understand how information shared online may be stored or
processed.

## 10.7 Intellectual Property and Ownership

The increasing use of generative AI raises important questions regarding
ownership, originality, and intellectual property.

Learners should develop an understanding of:

- authorship;

- ownership of creative work;

- attribution;

- copyright considerations;

- responsible reuse of content.

The curriculum encourages learners to use AI-generated content ethically
and to acknowledge AI assistance where appropriate.

## 10.8 Academic Integrity

Generative AI should support learning rather than replace learning.

The curriculum promotes the use of AI tools to:

- generate ideas;

- explore concepts;

- improve understanding;

- receive feedback;

- support creativity;

- assist with problem-solving.

AI tools should not be used to bypass learning processes or misrepresent
work as entirely self-generated.

Learners should be encouraged to demonstrate:

- understanding;

- originality;

- reflection;

- personal contribution.

Academic integrity remains an essential expectation across all learning
activities.

## 10.9 Responsible Prompting

Prompting is a key skill within modern AI literacy.

Learners should be encouraged to create prompts that are:

- clear;

- respectful;

- purposeful;

- specific;

- appropriate to the intended task.

Responsible prompting also includes considering how instructions may
influence outputs and recognising the importance of ethical
communication when interacting with AI systems.

## 10.10 Generative AI Across the Curriculum

The role of generative AI evolves throughout the curriculum.

| **Phase** | **Generative AI Focus**                                    |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Years 3–4 | Guided exploration and observation                         |
| Years 5–6 | Prompt refinement and output evaluation                    |
| Years 7–8 | Research support and workflow development                  |
| Year 9    | Critical evaluation, analysis, and responsible application |

This progression ensures that learners develop increasing independence
while maintaining appropriate safeguards and expectations.

## 10.11 Responsibilities of Learners

Learners are expected to:

- use AI tools responsibly and respectfully;

- verify important information;

- protect personal information;

- acknowledge AI support where appropriate;

- demonstrate academic integrity;

- consider ethical implications of AI use;

- reflect critically on AI-generated outputs.

## 10.12 Responsibilities of Teachers

Teachers are expected to:

- model responsible AI use;

- provide appropriate supervision;

- promote critical evaluation of AI outputs;

- encourage ethical discussion;

- support safe digital practices;

- integrate AI tools purposefully within learning activities.

Teachers should emphasise that AI is a learning tool rather than a
substitute for thinking, creativity, or effort.

## 10.13 Responsible Use Statement

The Artificial Intelligence Curriculum Framework promotes the
responsible, ethical, and informed use of generative AI technologies.

Learners are encouraged to engage with AI systems as thoughtful,
critical, and reflective users who understand both the opportunities and
challenges associated with emerging technologies.

By developing habits of verification, ethical reasoning, and responsible
participation, learners are better prepared to navigate an increasingly
AI-enabled future.
