# Teacher Guidance

## 14.1 Purpose

The Artificial Intelligence Curriculum Framework is designed to support
teachers in delivering high-quality, engaging, and developmentally
appropriate AI education to learners in Years 3–9.

Recognising that teachers may have varying levels of experience with
artificial intelligence, this guidance provides a shared pedagogical
approach that promotes consistency while allowing flexibility in lesson
design and delivery.

The guidance should be considered alongside the Scheme of Work, lesson
resources, and school policies. It is intended to support teachers in
creating inclusive, inquiry-based learning experiences that encourage
curiosity, critical thinking, collaboration, and responsible use of AI
technologies.

## 14.2 The Role of the Teacher

Within this curriculum, the teacher is not expected to be the sole
source of knowledge. Instead, the teacher creates opportunities for
learners to investigate, question, experiment, and reflect while
providing appropriate guidance and support.

Teachers are encouraged to adopt the following roles:

| **Role**          | **Description**                                                                                                        |
|-------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Facilitator       | Guides inquiry, discussion, and exploration rather than delivering information exclusively through direct instruction. |
| Learning Designer | Plans meaningful learning experiences that connect AI concepts with authentic contexts and learner interests.          |
| Coach             | Supports learners in overcoming challenges, refining ideas, and improving solutions through questioning and feedback.  |
| Ethical Guide     | Encourages thoughtful discussion about fairness, privacy, bias, responsibility, and the societal impact of AI.         |
| Assessor          | Uses formative assessment to monitor progress, provide feedback, and inform future learning.                           |

## 14.3 The Role of the Learner

Learners are active participants in the learning process. They are
expected to investigate questions, explore ideas, test solutions, and
reflect on their learning.

Throughout the curriculum, learners progressively develop greater
independence and responsibility.

Learners are encouraged to act as:

- investigators who explore how AI systems work;

- problem-solvers who apply AI to authentic challenges;

- collaborators who share ideas and learn from others;

- creators who design AI-supported solutions;

- evaluators who question AI outputs and justify decisions;

- reflective learners who identify strengths, challenges, and next
  steps.

## 14.4 Pedagogical Approach

The curriculum is underpinned by a combination of complementary teaching
approaches that reflect current best practice in STEAM and technology
education.

### Inquiry-Based Learning

Learning begins with meaningful questions and authentic problems that
encourage curiosity and investigation.

Teachers should encourage learners to ask questions such as:

- How does this AI system make decisions?

- Why did the AI produce this response?

- How could we improve the result?

- Is this output fair or reliable?

### Project-Based Learning

Each year includes a final project that enables learners to apply
knowledge and skills in a meaningful context.

Projects should:

- address authentic challenges;

- integrate multiple curriculum domains;

- encourage creativity and collaboration;

- include opportunities for reflection and improvement.

### Experiential Learning

Learners develop understanding through direct interaction with AI
systems rather than observation alone.

Teachers should provide opportunities for learners to:

- experiment with AI tools;

- compare outputs;

- investigate system behaviour;

- evaluate results.

### Reflective Learning

Reflection is an essential component of AI education.

Teachers should encourage learners to explain:

- what they observed;

- why outcomes occurred;

- how solutions could be improved;

- what ethical considerations should be taken into account.

## 14.5 Questioning Strategies

Effective questioning promotes deeper thinking and supports the
development of AI literacy.

Teachers should use a balance of open and closed questions,
progressively increasing cognitive demand.

Examples include:

### Recall

- What is AI?

- What information did the system use?

### Understanding

- How did the AI make its decision?

- Why did the result change?

### Application

- How could this AI tool help solve this problem?

- Which prompt would produce a better outcome?

### Analysis

- What patterns do you notice?

- Why might two AI systems produce different answers?

### Evaluation

- Is this response reliable?

- What evidence supports your judgement?

### Creation

- How would you improve this AI solution?

- What would you design differently?

## 14.6 Facilitating Ethical Discussions

Ethical conversations should occur naturally throughout learning rather
than being restricted to dedicated lessons.

Teachers are encouraged to promote respectful discussion and
evidence-based reasoning by asking questions such as:

- Who benefits from this AI system?

- Could anyone be disadvantaged?

- Is the information trustworthy?

- Should AI make this decision?

- What responsibilities do people have when using AI?

Teachers should create an environment where learners can explore
different viewpoints respectfully while recognising that ethical issues
often involve multiple perspectives.

## 14.7 Differentiation

The curriculum is designed to support learners with diverse needs,
interests, and prior experiences.

Differentiation should focus on providing appropriate support and
challenge rather than lowering expectations.

### Support

Teachers may provide:

- scaffolded instructions;

- worked examples;

- prompt templates;

- guided questioning;

- collaborative activities.

### Core

Learners apply curriculum expectations independently with appropriate
teacher guidance.

### Challenge

Teachers may extend learning by encouraging learners to:

- investigate more complex problems;

- compare multiple AI systems;

- analyse larger datasets;

- justify solutions using evidence;

- explore additional programming tasks in Years 7–9.

## 14.8 Common Misconceptions

Teachers should anticipate and address common misconceptions as learners
progress through the curriculum.

| **Misconception**                                | **Teaching Consideration**                                                                       |
|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| AI always gives correct answers.                 | Encourage verification using trusted sources and evidence.                                       |
| AI understands information like humans.          | Explain that AI identifies patterns rather than possessing human understanding or consciousness. |
| Larger datasets always produce better AI models. | Discuss data quality, representativeness, and bias.                                              |
| AI systems are unbiased.                         | Explore how training data and design decisions influence outcomes.                               |
| AI can replace all human decision-making.        | Reinforce the importance of human judgement, accountability, and ethical oversight.              |

## 14.9 Feedback

Feedback should be timely, specific, and focused on improvement.

Effective feedback should help learners:

- understand what has been achieved;

- identify areas for development;

- refine prompts, models, or solutions;

- improve reasoning and communication;

- reflect on ethical considerations.

Feedback should promote iteration rather than simply judging
performance.

## 14.10 Assessment in Practice

Teachers should gather evidence of learning through a range of
approaches.

Examples include:

- observation of practical activities;

- questioning during discussions;

- analysis of AI interactions;

- review of learner portfolios;

- project checkpoints;

- peer assessment;

- self-assessment;

- reflective journals;

- presentations and demonstrations.

Assessment should reflect the curriculum’s emphasis on understanding,
application, evaluation, and responsible participation.

## 14.11 Supporting Collaborative Learning

Collaboration is an important aspect of AI learning.

Teachers should encourage learners to:

- share ideas respectfully;

- justify decisions using evidence;

- review and improve one another’s work;

- divide responsibilities effectively during projects;

- recognise the value of diverse perspectives.

As learners progress through the curriculum, collaborative tasks should
gradually provide greater opportunities for learner independence.

## 14.12 Encouraging Responsible AI Use

Teachers should model the responsible use of AI by:

- demonstrating how to verify AI-generated information;

- discussing the strengths and limitations of AI systems;

- encouraging learners to acknowledge AI assistance where appropriate;

- reinforcing digital safety and privacy expectations;

- promoting ethical decision-making throughout learning.

Responsible AI use should become a routine element of classroom practice
rather than a standalone topic.

## 14.13 Indicators of High-Quality AI Teaching

The following indicators describe effective implementation of the
curriculum.

High-quality AI teaching:

- promotes curiosity and inquiry;

- encourages learners to question AI outputs;

- provides regular opportunities for practical investigation;

- integrates ethical discussion throughout learning;

- values experimentation and iterative improvement;

- connects learning to authentic contexts;

- supports collaboration and communication;

- encourages reflection on both process and outcome;

- develops conceptual understanding alongside practical competence;

- prepares learners to use AI confidently, responsibly, and critically.

## 14.14 Professional Learning and Continuous Improvement

Artificial intelligence is an evolving field, and teachers are
encouraged to engage in ongoing professional learning to maintain
confidence and currency.

Schools should support teachers by providing opportunities to:

- explore emerging AI tools and technologies;

- share effective classroom practice;

- collaborate on curriculum development;

- reflect on learner outcomes;

- review and refine teaching approaches in response to technological
  developments.

A collaborative culture of professional learning helps ensure that the
curriculum remains dynamic, relevant, and responsive to change.

## 14.15 Teacher Guidance Summary

Effective implementation of the Artificial Intelligence Curriculum
Framework depends upon knowledgeable, reflective, and adaptable teachers
who create engaging learning experiences while modelling responsible AI
use.

By combining inquiry-based pedagogy, practical application, ethical
discussion, and continuous reflection, teachers can support learners in
developing the confidence, competence, and critical understanding needed
to participate successfully in an increasingly AI-enabled world.
